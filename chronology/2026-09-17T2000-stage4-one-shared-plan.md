## The round

Three candidate patches. Removal surveys and proposes one. Ecology surveys and
proposes one with a fixed native mix. The room reveals, discusses, may revise,
then commits once. Shared removal saves a clearing charge. A small grant and
removal earnings fund restoration. Negative balances block commitment.

Solo testing switches from Removal to Ecology to Room automatically. Teams
provides two separate links for one laptop per team. The facilitator retains
reveal, commit and replay. Team proposals stay private until reveal. Decisions
are shared through a small server-side room; cameras and playback are local.
Rooms survive page reloads, not server restarts. No accounts or internet service.

No kiln placement, multi-patch routing, adjustable native mix, additional
missions, sensors or composite forest-health score. The money is game credits,
not claimed carbon-credit income. Native damage is a percentage of the removal
patch's native cover, not of the entire forest. Rules and caveats are documented
in `stage4/SHARED_ROUND.md`; patch descriptions and costs are editable in
`stage4/round-config.json`.

## Changes within the cloud

B has an authored broad opening; C has a narrower gap. The optional structure
lab uses corresponding qualitative profiles. These are training assignments,
not disturbance diagnoses inferred from the measured scan.

Recovery fades low growth and a native-damage strip. Relocated measured canopy
returns rise inside the restored patch. The ten-year transition is an
illustration of successful establishment, not species-specific growth.
Future labels show the fixed native mix; the study lab is explicitly labelled
Original structure during outcomes.

Fire runs on the existing educational Rothermel arrival solver. Both versions
use the same spark, wind and horizon. Optional 15 m fuel fields make irregular
paths across plot boundaries; the separate memory lab retains its original
behaviour by default. Fuel and moisture are authored, not classified from
LiDAR colour. Restoration assumes successful care and wetter litter. Removal
without restoration leaves some future fuel. This is not a historical fire
reconstruction or a calibrated forecast.

The front warms the actual points and adds small rising points; brown returns
retain the scar. With plan / Without plan compares the same time and viewpoint.
The recovery and fire views remain draggable and support close inspection.
The Canvas2D fallback shares geometry and arrival arrays, with fewer points and
no rising ember layer.

## Play and visual iteration

Used the browser skill. The in-app browser inventory was empty, so used the
documented fallback to local Playwright and inspected saved screenshots.
Played solo proposals, separate team sessions, a deliberately unaffordable
proposal revised into a shared patch, replay and phone-size no-WebGL play.

The first playthrough exposed stale-revision conflicts between independent
team surveys. Fixed those without allowing stale commitments or one team to
overwrite the other's proposal. Removed a redundant Keep proposal button.

The first fire was too dark. Brightened the front and retained a more readable
brown scar. The next screenshots showed square-edged burns, so replaced
whole-plot uniform fuel with irregular fine-scale bands. Compared recovery,
fire, no-plan scar and restored scar screenshots again. The final authored
example burns 10.4 ha without intervention; restoration at the narrow link
limits it to 0.8 ha within the chosen horizon. These are model outputs, not
estimated real losses. The higher-income patch has higher native damage;
there is no single score silently choosing a winner.
