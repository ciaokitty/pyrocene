# From spectral variation to one field-tested signature

The earlier hyperspectral cuts made two different mistakes. One enlarged a
small paper panel until its detail became hard to trust. Another drew a light
shape that looked like a measured canopy gap even though the source did not
contain one. The replacement keeps the useful idea and removes that false
precision.

Barbosa and colleagues provide the complete evidence chain in wet rainforest
at Wao Kele O Puna on the Island of Hawaii. Their CAO-AVIRIS imagery records 427
channels from 380 to 2510 nanometres. Canopy openings can allow lower leaves to
contribute to a mixed airborne pixel. Their target signature was trained from
field-identified strawberry-guava crowns, and their published BSVM map shows
where that signature repeats around a linked field site.

The 34 second experiment first holds on the published false-color rainforest.
It then selects the field site and keeps the camera on that footprint while the
image dissolves into the published similarity result. The result is recolored
with the existing cyan and magenta film palette but is not generatively
upscaled. The final sentence states the next scientific boundary: spectral
identity is evidence of species, not fuel.

The cyan locator is not described as a measured canopy gap. The gap sentence
explains the mixed-signal mechanism reported by the study; the following field
site and classifier supply the species evidence. This distinction is recorded
in the manifest, evidence-boundary document and release record.

The experiment passed a complete frame decode and a full-resolution visual
review. Its master is
`/mnt/seagate/videos/pyrocene/rainforest-continuity/experiments/spectral-openings-v1.mp4`.
The frozen 56 second LiDAR film and the earlier active candidates were not
modified.
