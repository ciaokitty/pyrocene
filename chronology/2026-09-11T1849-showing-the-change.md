# Why the room could not see what changed

The first playable version resolved a whole round behind one button. Press it,
and the night's removal, the lantana growth, the vote and the fire all happened
at once. The map ended up correct and nobody in the room could say what had
happened or why.

Three separate problems were hiding behind that, and they are worth separating
because only one of them was a design mistake.

## One: the round is two moments, not one

In the room, the night and the vote are different events with different
audiences. People sleep, wake, and hear a verdict. Then they argue and vote.
Collapsing both into one press meant the map moved twice while the room was
looking away.

Split into `finish night` and `finish vote`. Each shows one card of explanation,
then one animation. Not a card per effect, not an animation per effect. One of
each, because the room can only follow one thing.

## Two: the animation played but the map did not move

Reported as "the lantana spreading is visible as a flash, but the map is not
changing", which was exact. Every animation frame was generated by calling
`view()` after the state had already been mutated, so all ten frames drew the
finished board. Ten frames, two distinct images: the board before anything was
sent, and the finished one repeated nine times.

The fix is a blend: take the board from before, the board from after, and a set
of cell indices to reveal, and emit a board that is the old one everywhere
except those cells. Then reveal in chunks. That gave the sequence we actually
wanted: haze everything except the affected cells, let them change over about
four seconds, then lift the haze and show the settled board.

Fire needed one more frame after that. The last frame still showed the
pre-burn map because the burn was applied after the final snapshot was taken.

## Three: two things were painting the same screen

Each button's HTTP reply carried a rendered snapshot, and so did the event
stream. The reply was generated before the animation finished, arrived after it,
and overwrote the live board with a stale one. The console looked frozen. Fixed
by making the event stream the only thing that ever paints, and letting button
replies return nothing but an acknowledgement. Worth stating as a rule: one
writer per surface.

## What the cards say, and do not say

Cards never name who was removed. In Mafia the not-knowing is the game, and a
projector that announces the eliminated player hands the room the answer.
Leaving it out also lets a lantana player lie about whether the ranger or
ecologist is still in play, which is the kind of thing the room should have to
work out.

The language was pulled back to plain statements throughout, on instruction.
A fire that ignites in dense lantana and runs into a trench is already
interesting. It does not need help.
