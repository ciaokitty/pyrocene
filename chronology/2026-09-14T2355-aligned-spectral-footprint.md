# Keeping one forest footprint on screen

The first rainforest hyperspectral insert looked too soft to trust. That was not
an encoding failure. Its source was a 494 pixel wide strip taken from a
publication figure and enlarged to fill a 1920 by 1080 frame. It also followed
a LiDAR transect from another study. The viewer had no stable landmarks to use
when the image changed.

The gap film failed for a more serious reason. Its yellow light shape was a
procedural explanatory layer centred in the frame. It was not derived from a
gap in the LiDAR returns. The graphic looked like a detected feature even though
it was not one. We retired the film rather than refine the effect.

The replacement uses Figure 3 from Asner et al 2008. The published figure shows
the processing chain for one 53 hectare rainforest stand in Hawaii Volcanoes
National Park. LiDAR minimum vegetation height is aligned with imaging
spectroscopy. The same footprint then appears as live and dead canopy
composition and as a final invasive tree detection. A common crop keeps the
orientation and every spatial feature fixed.

We recoloured those published pixels into the existing green cyan and magenta
film palette. We did not create points or use generative upscaling. The source
map still has the spatial resolution of the paper figure. The improvement is
that it is shown honestly and can be compared across layers.

The first part of the insert still uses Seely et al 2025 LiDAR transects grouped
by measured strawberry guava abundance. That opening and the Asner map are
separate Hawaiian studies. The transition between them is conceptual. Once the
Asner map appears the three following layers are co-registered.
