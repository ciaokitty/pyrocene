# Handing the post-game film to two agents at once

The last piece is a closing sequence. A button at the end of a game that plays
30 to 60 seconds of what just happened, so the room watches their own evening
back rather than reading a summary.

Two agents were to build competing versions in parallel, on the same data. That
is a coordination problem, not a rendering one, and almost all the work went
into making sure neither of them needs to touch anything the other is touching.

## What made it work

**The event log became the contract.** Every round already wrote itself to disk
as it happened: who went out, every square that changed with its before and
after, the resilience action and its cells, and the fire with its ignition
square, cause, severity, wave-by-wave spread, burned squares and the trench
edges it pushed against. Plus the full 264-cell terrain and the player list.
That is a complete description of an evening. A renderer needs nothing else,
and specifically needs no game code.

**A real game was frozen as a fixture.** Not hand-written test data. An actual
7-night game, seed 13, 14 players, health falling 88 to 45, seven fires with
burns of 3, 4, 2, 20, 71, 3 and 18 squares, three of them pushing against a
trench, ending in a win with the forest at 45%. The 71-square night is the one
that matters: it is what a game looks like when lantana has been allowed to
connect up. Writing the specification against a real log rather than an
imagined one caught several fields that were named wrong.

**Every shared file was written in advance.** The button, the server routes
that list and serve simulations, the path containment on static serving, the
specification, the fixture. The agents get a directory each and nothing else,
so they cannot collide.

## What we asked for, plainly

A rendering of the gameboard that connects a room to what happened to their
forest. That is the whole brief. It replays a log of a board game about
invasive species and fire, using data the game itself wrote, and the
instructions say so in as many words. Being explicit about intent is not
overhead here, it is the thing that lets a capable model spend its effort on
the animation rather than on working out what is being asked.

Nothing in this entry has been reviewed yet. Both agents were still working when
it was written.
