# Real fires on the game's board

The fire lab burned the sample game's board and the fire was, honestly, not
bad enough to make the point. Real fires are. So two documented fires where
an invasive plant was the fuel were laid out on the game's board, in the
game's words, with the reported wind and dryness plugged in, and burned by
the same models twice: as it happened, and with the management the people
who work there actually use.

Open `/simulation/claude/lab/` and pick the case from the menu at the top.

## Bringing a real place into a 22 by 12 board

A case is a Python function in `lab/cases.py` that draws the landscape
onto the board with a seeded random generator, so the same case always
looks the same. It chooses the size of a square so the real distances fit,
names what each of the game's covers stands for there, gives the models the
case's wind and fuel table, and writes down its plan. The page shows the
board drawn exactly as the game draws one and a single line of facts
(place, date, fuel, square size, wind reported and modelled, duration,
sources). It said more at first; the words came out because a room does not
read them.

**Lahaina, Maui, 8 August 2023.** One square is 100 m. The town is the west
edge, the abandoned sugar and pineapple fields climb east into the hills,
the bypass runs between. "Thick lantana" stands for dense cured guinea grass,
"forest" for green yards and remnant trees, two dry gulches are bare. The
fire starts beside the bypass, where the afternoon fire started at 2:55 pm.
In the model it reaches the first lots in about ten minutes, which is what
happened. Homes are not fuel in the models, so what the hour shows after
that is the grass fire sweeping the fields along the whole town edge: 96
squares in ForeFire, 49 in Cell2Fire. A first pass with less wind and
forty minutes stopped short at the town, which read as a small fire; the
real one was not. The plan is the one Hawaii's wildfire organisation asks
for: a fuel break along the town edge (one square, 100 m, generous against
their rule of two to three times the fuel height) and the three rows of
fields nearest the town grazed short. Same fire, started on the nearest grass
left: 5 squares, held at the break.

**Bandipur Tiger Reserve, 21 to 25 February 2019.** One square is 200 m and
three hours are shown. Dry deciduous forest with lantana under it, dense on
38 percent of squares and moderate on another 50, matching the reserve's
own survey; a highway and a forest road; a tank; a village at the
south-west corner where the fire was set. As it happened, ForeFire burns 84
percent of the board in three hours and Cell2Fire 73. The plan is the forest
department's: fire lines along the highway and the forest road, lantana
cleared one square either side. Both models then hold the fire in the
south-west block: 27 percent.

## What had to be plugged in, and why

The wind is the honest compromise. Lahaina reported 18 m/s sustained with
gusts past 30; Bandipur 25 km/h. Rothermel's wind term with cured grass at
the reported wind gives a kilometre a minute, well past what was seen, so
the models are given 6 m/s at Lahaina and 2 at Bandipur, chosen so the first
building catches at about ten minutes in Lahaina and the fields still burn
out along the town edge within the hour. The page says both
numbers, reported and used, on the same line. Fuel loads and moistures are
in the case file with the source they lean on (guinea grass dead load
0.15 to 2.6 kg/m² on Oahu; lantana the game's own table).

Two more things the runs taught. ForeFire's front settings are in metres
and must scale with the pixel, or a 200 m board asks for a front resolved
at 1 m and never finishes; they now scale with the pixel size. And a
three-hour ForeFire run can exceed the subprocess timeout, which used to
kill the whole batch; it is caught now.

Gamba grass in the Northern Territory (four metres tall, eight times the
intensity of native grass) and the cheatgrass Martin Fire in Nevada
(435,000 acres) were read about and not built: they are bigger than a
board and their lesson is the same as Lahaina's.
