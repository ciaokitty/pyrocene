# One bearing from a canopy gap to a forest model

The earlier resolution experiments put useful layers beside each other, but a
film still needed to explain why the audience was moving from one layer to the
next. We made the final camera pose of the frozen LiDAR film the starting pose
of a new sequence. The camera keeps the same minus 31 degree bearing for the
entire shot. It rises and pulls back but never orbits the map.

The first layer marks fine violet spectral patterns only around structural
openings derived from the Amazon LiDAR. That fine texture comes from a real one
metre NEON observation in California, so the film permanently calls it an
educational composite. It explains what drone-scale spectroscopy could do. It
does not pretend a drone flew over this Amazon plot.

The selected area then lifts into an airborne-scale plane and widens into the
real NASA EMIT footprint. EMIT contributes measured Amazon reflectance at 60
metres. The twenty violet cells are the cells most similar to one seed in a
six-component representation of 211 retained reflectance bands. Keeping the
cells blocky is part of the explanation: the satellite can screen a forest but
cannot localize individual plants within those pixels.

The final transition turns spectral similarity and structural-opening proximity
into a smooth heat surface. This is deliberately labelled as an illustrative
model, not measured fuel load and not calibrated fire risk. The visual claim is
only that composition could become one input to a later fire-risk model.

Two edits preserve the same evidence. The 36 second guided cut gives each scale
its own caption. The 30 second continuous cut removes one beat and is intended
to feel like a direct continuation of the frozen film. Neither has been spliced
into the 56 second master.
