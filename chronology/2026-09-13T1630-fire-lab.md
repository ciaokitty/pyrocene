# Board, then a real fire model, then a picture: the fire lab

Two complaints about the night-forest film, both fair. The camera cut too
often to follow, and the fire was not simulated: it was the game's own
breadth-first flood, dressed up. The second one led to an afternoon's
experiment: take the board as it was when the big fire started, hand it to
real fire-spread models, and put their outputs side by side so someone can
judge which picture teaches. If none of them does, the experiment is over and
nothing is lost.

Everything lives in `stage2/simulation/claude/lab/`, with the built models and
raw outputs under `/mnt/seagate/models/pyrocene/lab/`. Open
`/simulation/claude/lab/?run=sample` on the stage 2 server.

## From board to landscape

One board square is thirty metres; the models run on a three-metre raster.
Every cover becomes a fuel class with Rothermel parameters, and the same table
drives every model, so they disagree only about how a front moves, never
about what is there to burn:

| class | load kg/m² | depth m | moisture | spread in a 3 m/s wind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| forest floor | 0.4 | 0.2 | 0.27 | 2.6 m/min |
| lantana, young | 0.6 | 0.5 | 0.11 | 23 m/min |
| lantana, spreading | 1.3 | 1.0 | 0.09 | 52 m/min |
| lantana, thick | 2.4 | 1.8 | 0.07 | 105 m/min |
| grass on bare ground | 0.08 | 0.08 | 0.22 | 1.5 m/min |
| water, homes, trench, char | nothing | | | 0 |

Every number is an assumption, chosen so that native forest floor barely
carries fire and thick lantana carries it fast, which is the claim the game
makes. Wind is assumed too (the log does not carry it). Hills raise the
ground thirty metres. The ignition square, the fuel and the trench are the
log's.

## The models, and what each one did

**The game itself.** The waves from the log, as the room saw them. 71 squares.

**A cell-to-cell automaton in the style of Cell2Fire** (own code, 120
lines): fire moves pixel to pixel, each pixel catching at the Rothermel rate
of its own fuel, with the elliptical wind bias Cell2Fire takes from the
Canadian FBP system and a slope term. Deterministic, a tenth of a second.
61 squares in thirty minutes, an elongated ellipse downwind through the
lantana, creeping into the forest floor at the edges. This is the one that
reads.

**ForeFire 2.5**, the model the brief pointed at. Runs from numpy arrays
through its Python binding, no files. Its continuous front is built for
smooth landscapes and this board is a thirty-metre checkerboard of fuel and
no fuel; the front fragments on the islands and collapses after six minutes,
with 29 squares burned. Three things had to be learned the hard way: the wind
direction layers must be shaped `(1, 2, nx, ny)` as its examples shape them,
or the fire runs the wrong way whatever the trigger says; the front must be
resolved finer than a pixel, or nodes meeting a no-fuel square are dragged
along it; and it prints an occasional NaN node, and a NaN inside a polygon
makes matplotlib's point-in-polygon test return whole bands of "burned"
pixels. That last one looked for two hours like a fire racing along the
river. Several instances in one process also fault, so each run is a
subprocess.

**Cell2Fire** (C2F-W, the real thing), built from source once libtiff and
Boost were fetched without root. It has its own fuel classes, so the board's
are mapped to Scott and Burgan models (TL1, SH1, SH5, SH9, GR1, NB9). Its cell
number is row times columns plus column and its wind bearing is where the
wind comes from; both were established by experiment. 30 squares in thirty
minutes, diamond-shaped from its eight-neighbour geometry, slower than the
others because its standard fuels are not the tuned table.

FARSITE and QUIC-Fire were not tried: FARSITE would need its Windows tooling
or a from-source Linux build for a result Cell2Fire and ForeFire already
bracket, and QUIC-Fire is not open.

## The counterfactual, and what it showed

Each model also burns the same board with the eleven squares the replay
marks as the critical night's joining squares cleared. Under the physics the
effect is modest: the automaton goes from 61 squares to 57, because the
stand's other pieces still touch spreading lantana, which burns nearly as
fast as thick. The game's severity rule counts thick squares in one
8-connected stand; a physical model counts continuous fuel of any kind. That
is worth knowing before bridging any of this into the film: the game's
critical night is a game fact, and the physics would want the whole corridor
cleared, or a break dug across it, to show a different fire.

## Second pass, the same evening

The first page had eight panels and too many words. Cut to six: the game's
own drawn map for reference, ForeFire and Cell2Fire, in two rows. The top
row is the board as played. The bottom row is a plan: clear the largest
connected stand of thick lantana, dig a fire line of ten squares along the
downwind edge of the lantana that is left, and start the same fire on the
nearest lantana still standing. The plan's map uses the game's own
iconography, so the room sees bare ground and a dashed trench exactly as
they saw them during play. Under this wind ForeFire burns 68 squares as
played and 13 with the plan; Cell2Fire 35 and 35, because its fire never
reaches the line. The counterfactual of clearing only the joining squares
was dropped: it did not change the physics and it was confusing.

Two more model lessons. ForeFire drops a front whole the moment it touches
the domain edge, which had looked like the fire going out at minute six on
every setting tried; a margin of nothing to burn around the board fixes it.
Cell2Fire's timber-litter classes hardly move at this scale, so the forest
floor is mapped to grass under trees (GR2) and its driest moisture scenario,
and it still burns slowly, in squares, because that is its geometry.

The forest floor was made more flammable (moisture 0.18, 10 m/min in this
wind) so the fire visibly leaves the lantana; the earlier setting kept it
inside. The slow camera orbit in the film was dropped at the same time as
too confusing; the bearing is fixed now, the easing between subjects stays.

## Where this stands

A page with six panels and one time slider: the board as played and the
board with a plan, each burned by ForeFire and Cell2Fire next to the game's
own map. The automaton still runs and is kept in the results file but is off
the page. Whether to drive the film's fire from a model is the next
decision, not made here.
