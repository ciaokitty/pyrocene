# Three readable scenes instead of one continuous flight

The first attempt to connect canopy gaps, measurement resolution and satellite
spectra treated the layers as one physical space. The camera rose through the
planes and into a top view. Every individual frame was defensible, but the
movement made the audience continually solve a changing viewpoint. The
explanation became harder than the three experiments it was meant to join.

We returned to the visual grammar that had already worked. The replacement is
three discrete scenes in this order: selected spectral signature, resolution
stack and Amazon EMIT at 60 metres. Each scene reaches a stable state and holds.
A one second dip through the existing near-black background separates the
camera spaces without introducing a new graphic device.

The selected-signature and EMIT shots still move. Their gradual lifts make the
relationship between oblique structure and the mapped footprint legible, but
their anticlockwise drift is limited to 3.5 degrees over the entire shot. The
resolution stack stays side-on and moves only 1.8 degrees. The audience inspects
the layers rather than travelling through them.

The final illustrative risk surface was removed from this edit. It can return
after the transition from spectral similarity to a defensible composition model
has its own explanation. This cut ends where the evidence is currently clearest:
whole 60 metre EMIT cells over the Amazon LiDAR footprint.
