# Human use and carbon context

This note supports the Stage4 field catalogue. It gives real examples of
human use and land management in Amazonia and the Guianas. It does not turn
the imagined settlement, plots or fire into historical observations.

## Evidence cards

### Food and non-timber livelihoods

Brazil nut is a major Amazonian non-timber forest product. Research with
Kayapó communities found that harvesting and moving seeds can affect seedling
recruitment in Brazil nut groves. A recent study of açaí extractivism records
fruit harvest as both food production and a source of household market income.
Buriti fruit is eaten and processed in Amazonian communities, while bacaba and
tucumã are also documented food and oil resources.

### Cultivation and domestication

Cassava is a staple crop in many Amazonian food systems. Guaraná is grown and
processed by Sateré-Mawé and other producers. Peach palm is a domesticated
Amazonian crop used for fruit and palm heart. Cupuaçu is cultivated for pulp
and seed products. These histories show active selection and management, not
an assumption that every forest patch is an untouched baseline.

Sources:

- [Origin and Domestication of Native Amazonian Crops](https://doi.org/10.3390/d2010072)

### Agriculture and fire coordination

In southeastern Amazonia, experiments linked repeated burning, canopy opening,
grass invasion and changes in fire intensity. A Carajás National Forest study
in Pará found lower natural regeneration where the introduced grass Urochloa
decumbens was present. Amazon Basin distribution research identifies Urochloa
brizantha and Urochloa decumbens as invasive species, while field research on
the southern Amazon rim documents invasion by Megathyrsus maximus after
disturbance and fire. Other Amazon fire research links ignition patterns to
land management. These studies support a cautious land-use mechanism. They do
not identify the ignition, grass species, fuel load or fire intensity in the
Stage4 scenario.

Sources:

- [Testing the Amazon savannization hypothesis](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0427)
- [The effect of exotic grass Urochloa decumbens in the Floresta Nacional de Carajás](https://doi.org/10.1590/0001-3765201620150121)

### Forest management and local knowledge

Extractive reserves and community managers use rules for Brazil nut, andiroba,
rubber and palm resources. Studies show that harvest intensity, access rights,
transport and local monitoring can change both household benefits and forest
outcomes. A species card should therefore distinguish documented use from an
assignment to a simulated plot.

### Voluntary carbon market and Verra

The voluntary carbon market is a market context, not a species scoring system.
Under the Verra Verified Carbon Standard, a Verified Carbon Unit represents
one tonne of project greenhouse gas reduction or removal after the required
project process. Verra describes a project-level sequence of methodology
selection, project description, validation by an approved validation and
verification body, monitoring, verification and issuance.

Project accounting must define a credible baseline and demonstrate
additionality. It must quantify project emissions or removals and specify
monitoring. AFOLU projects also need treatment of non-permanence and leakage
under the applicable program rules and methodology. These requirements apply
to a project boundary and monitoring plan. They do not award carbon credits
to a species, a photograph, a single plot or a field-guide observation.

This training material is not a carbon project, a credit claim, investment
advice or legal advice. No VCU value or eligibility is inferred from the
catalogue or the historical evidence layer.

### Community and biodiversity safeguards

Carbon and conservation projects can have community and biodiversity claims
that require their own evidence, participation and validation. A species list
can help a learning exercise ask better questions. It cannot establish land
rights, consent, benefit sharing, biodiversity outcomes or compliance with a
carbon standard.

Source:

- [Verra Climate, Community and Biodiversity Standards](https://verra.org/programs/ccbs/)

## Catalogue boundary

Taxon identity, native or invasive status and documented human uses are linked
to the cited sources. Plot membership, observed dryness, ignition, spread,
crew action and mission outcomes are scenario data. Medicinal-use records are
historical or cultural evidence and are not instructions for treatment.
