# A foldout for Pyrocene

Brainstorm context of 20 to 21 September 2026, for whoever designs the printed
piece next. Nothing here is built. No copy below is final. Read `## The
correction` before anything else, because an earlier draft of this brief got the
framing wrong in a way that is easy to repeat.

## What we are copying

A printed foldout for a coffee estate, photographed at
`/home/beeps/.claude/uploads/dc8ac693-83c2-4566-99be-d22ed6973879/`:
`cf52e667-image.jpg` (cover), `5fc536b0-image.jpg` (how to play),
`692303dc-image.jpg` (level 1), `0dc4a93d-image.jpg` (level 2).

It is an NES fighting-game pastiche. *The Salawara Bros. vs BerryBorer.* Two
named real farmers on a blue panel, a pixel-art coffee berry borer on a red one,
a START button repeated in Urdu and Hindi. Inside, a character roster with
epithets, a Super Mario World level map with five nodes, and one panel per level
of the actual coffee pipeline: Harvesting, Sorting, Fermentation, Drying, and a
boss level about farm design.

Four things make it work, and all four transfer:

- **The villain is a practice, not a creature.** The enemy is commodity coffee.
  The borer is only its consequence. "The bug follows breakdown. But the Bros.
  offer none."
- **Two health bars carry the arc.** The borer sits at 4/5 on level 1 and 3/5 on
  level 2. You can read the story from the bars without reading a word.
- **Real technical content in a toy frame.** Brix testing, dual flotation,
  microbial imbalance. The NES frame buys permission for the density.
- **Every level is a real stage of a real pipeline, in order.**

## The correction

An earlier draft of this brief proposed that the villain be fuel connectivity,
drawn as pink blobs that fuse into one band across the panels, with the boss
level being "cut the band".

That is wrong, and the reason is worth recording. `narrative/GAME_DESIGN.md` §2
states the project's one idea as *fire is not the problem, connected fuel is the
problem*. That sentence is correct for the Mafia evening in stages one to three.
It is too small for Stage 4, which is now about restoration economics, the years
in which a planting cannot yet survive alone, dispersal, livelihoods and payment
rules. Connectivity is one rule inside that, not the thesis.

The goal is **durable forest**. Reduced wildfire is one of several good
consequences of durable forest, not the objective.

### Climate change is the difficulty, not the villain

It is the force behind drier invasives, more frequent fire and other
disturbance. Keep it off the cover anyway:

- The project's rule is that the lesson is a mechanic and never a caption.
  "Climate change" printed on a red panel is a caption.
- It puts the one thing the player cannot affect into the slot marked *beat
  this*. Nothing done on this island moves the climate.

Where it lives instead is the **composition of the season deck** (see
`v2_strategies.md` §5). It sets how often the dry season comes and how much goes
wrong inside the vulnerability window. Early missions deal one dry season in
six; later ones deal two, then three. The deck is face up, so a room can count
the dry seasons in the decade ahead. Nobody says why. The facilitator says why,
if asked.

### There is no boss

Coffee has a pest, so that foldout has a boss. Restoration has no pest. It has a
finish line most projects never reach.

Level five is a **handover**: the season where nobody works that patch and it is
still there. The line is *a forest you have to keep saving is not restored.*

For the print piece this is a gift. Every other panel has the crew in it. The
last panel has no character at all, just forest, with the corner counter reading
**NIGHTS ×0**. The genre puts a hero in every frame, so breaking that is the win
state.

## The structure to print

### Health bars

Not hero versus villain. **NEEDS YOU** on the left and **HOLDS ITSELF** on the
right, with six patches distributed between them. Patches migrate right panel by
panel. This is the care ledger, drawn, so the foldout and the game teach the
same object.

### Cover

No creature on the red panel. A fan of face-down cards: dry year, grass, cattle,
drought, and the funder's receipt. Not one enemy, a deck.

Working title **MAKE IT HOLD**. The title should name the goal, since there is
nobody to defeat.

### The spine

Drop the Mario world map. The project already has a better one: the hand-drawn
board, with five numbered stops on it. The board is a 6×6 grid of 264 squares, so
pixel art of the real map is honest and distinctive rather than borrowed. Six map
styles exist; see `stage2/maps/README.md`.

### The five panels

These lines are approved and should be retained.

| Level | Title | The line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | A satellite cannot see a seedling |
| 2 | Removal | A job half done becomes a corridor |
| 3 | Replant | You cannot plant a canopy. You plant what makes one possible |
| 4 | Tend | Planting is not an outcome. It is an obligation with a closing date |
| 5 | Handover | A forest you have to keep saving is not restored |

Connectivity belongs in level 2, as one consequence of clearing badly. That is
its right size.

Each panel wants a paragraph of real content at the density of the reference's
brix-testing text. Sources for that paragraph, per level: level 1 from the
online game's look-or-act rule (`narrative/GAME_DESIGN.md` §8); level 2 from the
corridor rules (§5); level 3 from pioneer-then-canopy succession
(`stage4/NEGLIGENCE.md` and the Freitas reference in it); level 4 from the
vulnerability window and its stressors (`stage4/v2_strategies.md` §§1–3); level 5
from what a durable forest gives back (`v2_strategies.md`, the benefits section).

### Corner counters

The reference uses flask ×3 and cherry ×3, rising per level. The Pyrocene
equivalent is **NIGHTS ×1**, because one action a night, look or act but never
both, is the rule the whole thing rests on. It reads ×0 on the handover panel.

### Format

One sheet, printed both sides, accordion-folded into four A5 panels gives eight
faces: cover, roster and map, five levels, back matter.

Two things the reference does not have belong on the back. First, the
declaration of which pixels are drawn and which came from a measured scan,
because that rule is the project's character (`GAME_DESIGN.md` §7 and §10).
Second, a blank grid, so the sheet doubles as the board a room marks up during
the physical-map activity in Stage 4.

## The second version, if two are wanted

The user asked about a socio-economic-ecological cut as an alternative. Its
antagonist is **the payment rule**: what gets measured gets paid. Draw it as a
stamp or clipboard, **VERIFIED**, whose only power is that it pays for what can
be weighed on the day of the work. Never a community.

Three heroes rather than two, which is the real triangle: the removal crew, paid
today; the ecologist, paid never; the keeper, paid only if it lasts.

| Level | Title | The line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The grant | Somebody will pay you to cut. Nobody will pay you to wait |
| 2 | Tonnes | It can be weighed, so it can be paid |
| 3 | The return | It grew back. It pays again |
| 4 | The window | You are holding more open ground than you can fund |
| 5 | Write the rule | They paid for the cutting. Nobody paid for the keeping |

The bars here are the policy game's own two numbers, credits and forest health,
and the payload is that they come apart. Rules and measured outcomes are in
`stage4/v1_policy_gaming.md`.

**Recommendation: print one piece, not two.** Levels one to four are the
pipeline; level five asks who pays. The pipeline alone is a poster. The
economics alone have nothing concrete to bite on. The pipeline earns the right
to ask the question.

## Rules the piece has to respect

These are not style preferences. They are why the project can be shown to people
who work in the field.

- **Declare what kind of thing every picture is.** Measured scan, modelled
  scenario, recorded game, drawn illustration. Never let the categories blur.
- **No invented person presented as a real practitioner.** The reference names
  two real farmers. If Pyrocene names anyone, they have agreed to it. Otherwise
  use roles. Hazel (`stage4/hazel.png`) and Lia (`stage4/lia-v2.png`) are
  existing illustrated characters and are not passed off as staff.
- **Do not label a plant the enemy by dominance alone.** See the Vismia caution
  in `stage4/REINFESTATION_DISCUSSION.md`.
- **Never claim a credit price or a VCM eligibility.** Credits in the game are
  authored teaching values.
- **Do not present the Amazon scan as Indian dry forest or the reverse.** Stages
  one to three are lantana country. Stage 4's geometry is measured Amazon
  (EBA T_0638, May 2017, Ometto et al., CC BY 4.0).

## Open questions

1. **Register.** The reference is knowingly goofy, with epithets like "The
   Fermentatic Fanatic". Pyrocene's written voice is plain and spare. Plain
   probably wins, but the alliteration is genuinely charming and this is
   marketing, not the game.
2. **Aesthetic.** Full NES pixel pastiche, or the game's own material (point
   cloud slabs, dark green, Georgia serif, the hand-drawn map) arranged in a
   foldout? A middle path is to pixel-art the real board, which keeps the joke
   and stays honest about the data.
3. **Languages.** The reference repeats START in Urdu and Hindi. Stage 4 runs on
   Amazon data while stages one and two are lantana country, so the honest set is
   probably English, Kannada and Portuguese. Mixing them carelessly
   misrepresents one landscape as the other.
4. **Who it is for.** A room about to play, a funder, or a practitioner
   audience? The reference is a farm's calling card. The level-five question
   lands very differently on a funder than on a room.
