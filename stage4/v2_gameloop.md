4 Fire prevention strategies

2. Canopy restoration
3. Fire breaks
4. Early warnings

Invasive removal and restoration are already a part of the gameloop. We can focus on that initially. 
The game loop is basically cooperatoin and negligance combined. 
Removal and restoration need to cooperate. 
And there is the added complexity of incremental development of the map - restored plots keep getting "reinvaded". 
Is it possible to actually have a "mode" that combines these? meaning you as the single player can go into either - to get funds you remove remove - and to raise forest health and pay for early warning etc you can use your earnings from removal? 
That might need a clear removal -> credits narrative. 

Bascially the idea is to have one or more of these turn into a sink for one of more of these and force the user to switch strategies by making real observations about the environment like invasvies, connectivity, where to put a fire break, and where to restore. 

Just like some famous games like AOE we might need to speed up time as restoration takes a longer time to take hold. 
So that is just a strategy of creating a long term in tact forest and not necessarily to prevent current fires.
There can also be a differential time dilation - first six months of restoration invasives keep coming back and then the user is allowed to jump in time to reap the benefits of their restoration. 
One thing that might be cool to teach is that you can't just restore the canopy by planting canopy species - there is a transition of pioneer species or first a group of species that establish and then a transition to the real canopy species. So in the restoration track you would have to be careful about what you choose to restore to a degree and learn by trail/error/deduction that directly planting a pioneer species just leads to low recruitment/survival. 
The vmc equivalent could be livelihoods in the local area. Assume the indigenous communities themselves are wanting to protect the forest from wildfire - is there an angle where they earn by doing "ecosystem services" funded by the government? or should we shift the focus to actually doing lifestyle activities? meaning its interesting today because the thing you do to make credits (remoe invasives) is in competitiont with the lower invasions in the restored plots - meaning in both cases it's "removal" but in the plot restoration case you are forced to reckon with the lower money value of removal in a restoration plot but the higher "forest health" value it eventually brings. That is a good dynamic to bring out.
